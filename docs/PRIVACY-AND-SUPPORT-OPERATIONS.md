## Purpose, necessity and balancing assessment

Proposed basis selected for launch copy: consent for optional launch updates/publicity; legitimate interests for proportionate adult community/application administration, project coordination, support, moderation and security; a specific legal obligation only when one actually applies. No invented contract necessity or blanket acceptance/marketing consent.

Purpose: maintain an accessible professional community, respond to a person's request, organise voluntary participation and protect participants. Necessity: contact details and relevant interests/availability support those tasks; the full application is not necessary for every team member or public visitor. Restrict project-lead access to relevant participation information, not the whole membership response.

Balancing: adult applicants reasonably expect an application to be reviewed and an issue to be considered, but do not reasonably expect public disclosure of phone numbers/application answers, unrelated promotion or indefinite retention. Risks include group-number visibility, unwanted outreach, misjudged moderation reports, copies of exports, excess biographical information and international access. Measures: clear notices; optional/unnecessary field review; no public application directory; role-based access; no automatic consequential decisions; separate marketing choice; rights/objection route; minimised evidence and scheduled deletion. Actual provider settings and administrator practice must match these measures before relying on this assessment. If a purpose fails the assessment, change the process or establish an appropriate basis before using the data.

## Retention schedule and rationale

- Unsuccessful/withdrawn applications: at most 12 months after decision/withdrawal, enabling clarification and handling a genuine follow-up without keeping speculative prospects indefinitely.
- Active member/project administration: while needed for participation; annual review; remove unnecessary administration within 12 months after participation ends. Separate repository history from membership administration.
- Waitlist: earliest of withdrawal when no longer needed, 30 days after final launch communication, or 12 months without relevant engagement. Do not use engagement as a pretext to extend indefinitely. Keep only a minimal justified consent/opt-out record where necessary; never keep the whole profile to honour an opt-out.
- Routine support: at most 12 months after closure, unless a specific unresolved/legal reason exists.
- Moderation: review on closure and no later than 24 months later. Delete/anonymise unless a documented, proportionate safety or legal need remains; set a further review date for each exception.
- Rate counters: hashed network entries pruned after one day on the next request; idle state can persist. Add a private periodic housekeeping task if automatic daily deletion is desired. Never delete the active lock/state file during a request.
- Provider logs/backups: record actual hosting/provider retention and deletion limitations. Do not invent a uniform period. Avoid restoring deleted working records without reapplying deletion.

These are manual operational duties for external forms, inboxes, spreadsheets and communities, not deletion automation delivered by the static website. Assign a named owner, carry out monthly retention housekeeping and maintain a minimal completion/exception log. Do not create automated launch emails in this change.

## Moderation workflow

Log the minimal facts privately; identify immediate risk and any conflict; restrict access temporarily if necessary; consider relevant evidence and affected parties fairly; record a proportionate response and rationale; communicate what can appropriately be shared. Offer a review on relevant further context, using another suitable administrator where practicable. No absolute confidentiality, independent tribunal or general support SLA is promised. Privacy deadlines remain separate. Serious emergencies belong with appropriate local services, not an unmonitored inbox.
